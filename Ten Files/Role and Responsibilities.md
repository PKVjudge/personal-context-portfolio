# Role and Responsibilities

## Core Responsibilities

My most visible and business-critical responsibilities are the success of the group’s Product Value Streams and the delivery of major projects. I am accountable for making sure the Value Streams remain steerable, aligned with strategic priorities, and able to move through organizational blockers. Because the organization is heavily loaded, portfolio prioritization, escalation management, and clear decision preparation are central parts of my role.

I am also responsible for strategic portfolio management, process management and process automation, IT governance, regulatory IT steering, IT contracts and their throughput, IT budget tracking and reporting, and the preparation of board-level decision materials. These areas matter operationally and regulatorily, but they are less central to my external visibility than Value Stream performance and project outcomes.

The Value Streams currently operate as a virtual organization. The Value Stream Leads report directly to me and provide content and lateral leadership across their streams, but the people working in the streams still sit in their line functions such as IT, risk management, marketing, operations, or other business units. Capacity therefore has to be negotiated with the line organization. Because the Value Streams have strong board relevance and because I have broad trust across the organization, we can often secure capacity despite this structural limitation.

A possible future development path is to connect Value Streams and product management more tightly and to move selected roles directly into the Value Stream setup. Product management is a likely starting point for this discussion.

## Weekly Cadence

My week is shaped by leadership alignment, portfolio steering, Value Stream coordination, board preparation, and escalation management.

- **Monday morning:** 1:1 with the Head of Process Management.
- **Tuesday evening:** CIO leadership jour fixe with Wolfgang, Christian Fuchs as Development division head and IT subsidiary managing director, Roland Freitag as IT Operations division head, and me.
- **Every second Tuesday:** Board meeting. Enterprise Steering reporting and project backlog prioritization are prepared for these meetings.
- **Thursday evening before each board meeting:** Deadline for finalizing board meeting documents.
- **Wednesday afternoon:** Weekly jour fixe with all of my direct leadership team.
- **Weekly:** Value Stream alignment with all Value Stream Leads, co-led with the transformation team and innovation staff unit.
- **Weekly:** 1:1s with the Value Stream Leads.
- **Friday:** 1:1 with the Head of IT Contracts and Business Organization.
- **Recurring:** Project-specific Steering Committees for larger initiatives, delivery risks, budget questions, and escalations.

I currently also have a monthly 1:1 with Wolfgang, the responsible executive board member for my area. He retires at the end of May. The operating rhythm with his successor will become one of the key factors shaping my future work, because the direct relationship with my new manager will strongly influence my mandate, visibility, and effectiveness.

## Monthly / Quarterly Rhythms

The most important recurring rhythm is the board and portfolio steering cycle. Every second board meeting includes Enterprise Steering reporting and project backlog prioritization. The portfolio management function prepares prioritization proposals based on business value, time value, and strategic fit together with division heads and strategy owners from across the group. The board makes the final decision, but well-prepared portfolio proposals are usually accepted with little change.

The organization also works with recurring Value Stream and project steering rhythms, including project-specific Steering Committees, Value Stream alignment routines, OKR and delivery tracking, and escalation formats. Quarterly planning and review cycles shape the work of the Value Streams and major initiatives, especially where capacity, dependencies, priorities, and delivery risks need to be aligned across functions.

In the coming months, the group’s future organizational setup will be an important additional rhythm. The new executive structure, the future role of the Value Streams, and the possible connection between Value Streams and product management will shape my mandate and operating model.

## Key Decisions

Many decisions in the organization are escalated to the board because of the current executive decision culture, even when they could theoretically be resolved at lower levels. I have a relatively broad formal mandate, but I use it selectively. A large part of my role is to prepare decisions so well that the board can approve them quickly and with minimal rework.

Regular decisions and judgments include:

- deciding which issues can be solved bilaterally and which need to be brought into a formal committee or board setting;
- shaping project prioritization proposals before they go to the board;
- assessing business value, time value, and strategic fit of project proposals together with other division heads and strategy owners;
- making or preparing project scope decisions;
- resolving prioritization conflicts within projects and across initiatives;
- judging when Value Stream priorities justify pushing for capacity from line functions such as IT, risk management, marketing, operations, or other business units;
- deciding how pragmatic or formal a governance or process path needs to be, depending on the people, risks, and organizational dynamics involved;
- reviewing budget questions and deciding when they can be handled operationally versus when they require executive alignment.

## What I Produce

My work produces decision-ready materials, steering transparency, and operating structures rather than one single type of deliverable. The most important outputs are:

- board papers and decision materials;
- Enterprise Steering reports for board meetings;
- portfolio prioritization lists and board-ready prioritization proposals;
- project status reports and Steering Committee materials;
- Value Stream and OKR reporting;
- IT budget tracking and reporting;
- project scope, escalation, and decision papers;
- presentations for the board, Management Board, division heads, and cross-functional steering groups;
- process documentation and process automation roadmaps;
- governance policies, committee rules, IT regulatory steering documents, and other formal steering artifacts.
